Sales Guide: Adtaxi Market Analyzers

A step-by-step walkthrough of the Adtaxi Market Analyzers and planning tools, from choosing a category to handing the client a proposal, and what the numbers do and don't mean.

Home

What the tools are for

The analyzers show a client or prospect that Adtaxi knows their industry and knows where in their market the best customers are. Use them to open the conversation, to show where the budget should go, and to leave the client with a proposal built on the same numbers.

There is one analyzer per business category: Apartments, Auto, Financial, Franchise Development, Furniture, Healthcare, Higher Education, Home Services, Legal and Travel. Every analyzer works the same way, so once you know one, you know them all. Three planning tools work alongside any category: the Trade Area Tool, Customer Analysis (CRM) and the TV-to-Digital Analyzer.

Follow the green highlight

Every page walks you through it. The box you need to fill in next is shaded light green with a teal edge and a Next tag. When you complete it, the highlight moves to the next step. Nothing is locked: you can work in any order, and the highlight follows whatever is still missing.

Part 1: Find the client's best markets

  1. Open the client's analyzer. From the home page, pick the client's category under Category analyzers, for example Home Services or Legal.
  2. Choose the category. The first highlighted box is the category list (Service, Practice area, Vehicle line and so on). Pick the client's line of business, for example Roofing and gutters or Personal injury.
  3. Choose a geography. Next, Geography is highlighted. Pick DMA, Metro or County to rank markets at that level. Leave Rank and map by on Market Potential Score unless you want to show total spending or households per square mile.
  4. Click the client's market. Click it on the map, click its row in the ranked list, or type its name in Search. The details panel shows where it ranks ("12th of 210 DMAs") and the four parts of the score. Point to the strongest bar, for example "households here spend 20% more than average on this service."
  5. See where in the market the best customers are. Click Look inside on the market's row to rank the metros, counties or ZIPs inside it. Use this to show which neighborhoods hold the most potential. Looking inside doesn't select anything; it's for exploring.
  6. Pick the areas the client covers. Tick the boxes beside the markets, counties or ZIPs the client serves (any mix), then click Open as trade area. Or click Trade area in Geography to start from a ZIP and a radius instead.

Along the top of every analyzer you'll also find: How scores work (the method and data dates), Your CRM footprint (Customer Analysis for this category), TV to Digital (appears once you're on a DMA, Metro or County view), the category slides and the subcategory report to share before or after a call, and Export for a leave-behind PDF or the table as a CSV. The Timing tab shows the category's busiest and slowest months, and Benchmarks shows typical cost per click, click-through, conversion rate and cost per lead by platform.

Part 2: Build the trade area and the plan

  1. Trade area. Use Radius for a business with one location: a ZIP or town and a distance. Use Custom area for a list, one item per line: ZIPs, counties ("Wake County, NC"), towns, DMAs, metros, states or Census regions. You can also upload a client's spreadsheet of ZIPs or counties. Anything the tool can't read is listed rather than guessed.
  2. Audience. Choose the target audience. Each category has a ready-made profile of its likely customers, such as homeowners in older homes for plumbing; change any filter to fit the client. The ranked ZIP list shows each ZIP's estimated audience and its index (100 is average; 150 means 50% more concentrated). When you're happy with the area, click Build a plan from these ZIPs.
  3. Plan. Optionally enter a goal: the percent of the audience to reach and how often ("40% reach at a 4 frequency"). Then choose a media strategy, such as Brand awareness or Lead generation. The monthly budget fills in with a suggested amount for the goal; type any budget to see what it buys, or click Use suggested to put the suggestion back.
  4. Recommended allocation. The budget is split across Adtaxi's platforms, each with one monthly cost and the reason it's recommended. Estimated monthly delivery shows targeted impressions, reach, frequency, TRPs, targeted cost per point and CPM, and whether the plan meets the goal. Click Results to show client case studies that match the plan's category and platforms.
  5. Proposal. Enter the client's name and click Build proposal to download an editable PowerPoint with the trade area, the recommended investment by platform and the estimated delivery.

The three ways to rank and map

ChoiceWhat it tells youUse it to say
Market Potential ScoreThe overall best places to put ad dollars, 0 to 100."These are the strongest markets for your business."
Total spendingHow big the market is in dollars a year."There's an estimated $780 million a year spent on this here."
Households per square mileHow many likely customers a service area or radius can reach."A 10-mile radius here reaches far more households than in a spread-out market."

Reading a market's details

The score's four parts. Consumer Demand is the size and need of the customer base. Category Spending is what households spend on the category. Spending Power is the share of households earning $75K or more. Competition is how few competing businesses there are per resident, so a higher bar means less competition.

Underserved. "Yes" means strong demand but few competitors per resident: a strong case for a client weighing a new location or service area.

Competitor locations. The checkbox on the map adds circles sized by the number of competing businesses in each market or ZIP. They are counts by area, not addresses.

Data notes. Some markets show notes under their details, such as estimated values or provisional boundaries. Read them before quoting that market's numbers.

Safe to say

  • "Your market ranks in the top 10% nationally for your category."
  • "These ZIPs have the highest concentration of your likely customers."
  • "Households here spend more than average on what you sell, according to EASI estimates."
  • "This market has fewer competitors per resident than most."

Don't say

  • That a score predicts leads, sales or revenue. Scores rank markets against each other; they aren't forecasts.
  • That spending figures are actual sales. They're EASI's modeled estimates of household spending.
  • That the competitor circles show specific businesses. They're Census counts by area.
  • That a radius count is exact when it combines filters. It's an estimate.
  • That a proposal's or the TV-to-Digital comparison's impressions, reach, frequency, clicks or leads are guaranteed. They're planning estimates from Adtaxi's rate card and published industry benchmarks.

Where the data comes from

Census American Community Survey (people, homes and income by ZIP), Census Business Patterns (competing businesses), EASI Demographics 2025 (household spending), CDC PLACES (health conditions), the Department of Education's IPEDS directory (schools), NOAA (climate and hail), IRS Statistics of Income (tax returns by ZIP), the 2026 AudienceSCAN® study (how each target group responds to advertising) and Scarborough (broadcast TV audiences in the TV-to-Digital Analyzer). Proposal estimates use the Adtaxi rate card and published benchmarks from WordStream, Innovid and the Annual Franchise Development Report.

Each analyzer's How scores work button explains its method, data dates and limits. Every data build checks the numbers against published national totals.
