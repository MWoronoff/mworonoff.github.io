Sales Guide: Adtaxi Market Analyzers

How to use the Healthcare, Legal, Higher Education and Home Services analyzers in a pitch, and what the numbers do and don't mean.

All analyzers

What the tools are for

The analyzers show a prospect or client that Adtaxi has data on their industry, analyzes it, and knows where in their market the best customers are. Use them to win the business and to back up where the budget should go. They don't split a budget for you; that's still your call, informed by what the tools show.

A five-minute walkthrough

  1. Open the analyzer for the client's industry and pick their category.For example, Home Services and then Roofing and gutters, or Legal and then Personal injury.
  2. Show where their market ranks.Leave Rank and map by on Market Potential Score, then type the client's DMA or metro in Search. "Your market ranks 12th of 210 for roofing" opens the conversation.
  3. Explain why, from the market's details.The four bars show what drives the score. Point to the strongest one, for example "households here spend 20% more than average on this service."
  4. Drill into ZIPs.Click See ZIPs in this market. The map shows which neighborhoods hold the most potential, so ad dollars can go there first.
  5. Size the audience around their location.Open the Zip Code Radius Tool, enter the client's ZIP, pick a radius and choose their category under Best prospects for. Export the ranked ZIP list for the media plan.

Reading a market's details

The score's four parts. Consumer Demand is the size and need of the customer base. Category Spending is what households spend on the category. Spending Power is the share of households earning $75K or more. Competition is how few competing businesses there are per resident, so a higher bar means less competition.

Underserved. "Yes" means strong demand but few competitors per resident: a strong pitch for a client weighing a new location or service area.

Competitor locations. The checkbox on the map adds circles sized by the number of competing businesses in each market or ZIP. They are counts by area, not addresses.

Data notes. Some markets show notes under their details, such as estimated values or provisional boundaries. Read them before quoting that market's numbers.

Using the radius tool

Best prospects for loads a ready-made profile of each category's likely customers, such as homeowners in homes built before 1980 for Plumbing. Change any filter to fit the client.

Index compares a ZIP's share of prospects with the national share; 100 is average and 150 means 50% more concentrated. The list is sorted so the most concentrated ZIPs come first. Sort by Est. audience to see the largest counts.

Audience counts that combine several filters are estimates, and the tool labels them that way.

Safe to say

  • "Your market ranks in the top 10% nationally for your category."
  • "These ZIPs have the highest concentration of your best prospects."
  • "Households here spend more than average on what you sell, according to EASI estimates."
  • "This market has fewer competitors per resident than most."

Don't say

  • That a score predicts leads, sales or revenue. Scores rank markets against each other; they aren't forecasts.
  • That spending figures are actual sales. They're EASI's modeled estimates of household spending.
  • That the competitor circles show specific businesses. They're Census counts by area.
  • That a radius count is exact when it combines filters. It's an estimate.

Where the data comes from

Census American Community Survey (people, homes and income by ZIP), Census Business Patterns (competing businesses), EASI Demographics 2025 (household spending), CDC PLACES (health conditions), the Department of Education's IPEDS directory (schools), and NOAA (climate and hail). Each analyzer's How scores work button explains its method, data dates and limits.

Every data build checks the numbers against published national totals. For Healthcare, Legal and Home Services, it also compares each category's scores with where Census finds that industry's employees, and flags any category where the two don't line up.
