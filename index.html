The Story

13 East 47th Street

In the late 1940s and early 1950s, The Panda operated at 13 East 47th Street in Manhattan, between Fifth and Madison Avenues. It was a restaurant and supper club offering cocktails, dinner, supper, music, entertainment and dancing.

Family history records that Mike (Michele) Ronzitti entered the restaurant business with Andy and Gus. Together they acquired an existing Chinese restaurant called The Panda and retained its established name and customer base.

Mike later bought out Andy and Gus and became sole proprietor. The restaurant became closely associated with Mike and the Ronzitti family, and surviving memorabilia, photographs and newspaper coverage provide fragments of its story.

The goal of this project is not to recreate what we wish The Panda had been, but to reconstruct what can actually be documented — one photograph, newspaper clipping, record and family memory at a time.
Family Story

Mike & Ester Ronzitti

Mike and Ester Ronzitti. Family photograph.

The family behind The Panda

Mike Ronzitti was born in Genoa, Italy, in 1910. Ester Kavish Ronzitti was born in Yekaterinburg, Russia, around 1915–1916.

Their lives eventually converged in New York City, where they married and raised their daughters, Nicoletta Sema and Susan.

The Panda was not simply a Manhattan restaurant. For the Ronzitti family, it became part of a much larger immigrant and family story — one that crossed countries, generations and neighborhoods before arriving at 13 East 47th Street.

The Ronzitti Home

308 West 97th Street

Away from the bustle of East 47th Street, the Ronzitti family made their home at 308 West 97th Street on Manhattan's Upper West Side.

308 West 97th Street, circa 1940. The tax placard reads “1887 - 26 M.” Enhanced and recentered presentation image based on the archival tax photograph.

Home on the Upper West Side

The family's residence at 308 West 97th Street, also historically identified as 306–308 West 97th Street, provides another physical anchor for the Ronzitti story in New York.

The property was historically Manhattan Block 1887, Lot 26. The apartment building was constructed in 1900–1901 and designed by architect Hugh Lamb.

The surviving tax photograph allows us to see the building as it appeared around the period when the Ronzitti family lived there, adding a residential counterpart to the surviving images of The Panda.

Sources & Verification

How this history is being reconstructed

Documented

Archival photographs, newspaper clippings, government records, the 308 West 97th Street tax photograph, and surviving Panda ephemera are treated as documentary evidence.

Family History

Details including Mike, Andy and Gus acquiring The Panda together, Mike later buying them out, and the Panda charms being handed out are presented as family-provided history unless independently documented.

Still Being Researched

Exact ownership dates, additional exterior photographs, interior photographs, a surviving menu and further business records remain active areas of research.

Storefront verification: the exterior photograph clearly shows the Panda name on the rounded canopy and “The Panda RESTAURANT” in the storefront window. The historical storefront photograph is treated as documentary evidence. Where an enhanced or recentered presentation image is used elsewhere on the site, that treatment is identified in its caption and the underlying archival image remains the evidentiary source.
Ongoing Research

Help us find more

We are continuing to search for photographs, menus, advertisements, matchbooks, postcards, newspaper stories, memorabilia and personal memories of The Panda Restaurant & Supper Club at 13 East 47th Street, particularly from approximately 1946–1951.

We are especially interested in interior photographs, additional exterior photographs, photographs of East 47th Street between Fifth and Madison Avenues, and an original Panda menu.

The archive will continue to change as additional material is located, evaluated and verified.

This site is a family historical research project. Family recollections are identified separately from documentary evidence whenever possible.
