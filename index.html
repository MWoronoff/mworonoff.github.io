The Story

A supper club in postwar Manhattan

In the late 1940s and early 1950s, The Panda operated at 13 East 47th Street in Midtown Manhattan, on the north side of the block between Fifth and Madison Avenues.

Family history records that Michele (Mike) Ronzitti entered the restaurant business with Andy and Gus. The three acquired an existing restaurant called The Panda and retained its name in hopes of keeping its established customers. Michele (Mike) later bought out Andy and Gus and became sole proprietor.

Surviving restaurant material shows that The Panda presented itself as more than a place to eat. Its promotional material advertised cocktails, dinner, supper, music, entertainment and dancing — the language of a small Midtown restaurant and supper club.

One of the restaurant's specialties: surviving Panda matchbooks advertise “Shrimp à la Panda.” Locating a surviving menu remains one of the project's highest research priorities.

Restaurant Branding

A panda was part of the identity

Surviving matchbooks show that the restaurant did not rely on the name alone. A panda illustration formed part of its visual branding.

The matchbooks display the Panda name, the East 47th Street address, the ELdorado telephone number and the phrase “Shrimp à la Panda.”

Family Photograph

Michele (Mike) & Ester Ronzitti

Michele (Mike) Ronzitti and his wife, Ester, are at the center of this family history.

Michele was born in Genoa, Italy, and Ester in Yekaterinburg, Russia. Their individual journeys eventually brought them to New York City, where they built a life together and raised their daughters, Nicoletta Sema and Susan.

In the late 1940s, Michele's story became intertwined with The Panda at 13 East 47th Street. For the Ronzitti family, the restaurant was not simply a Midtown business; it became part of the family's history.

Marriage record

The marriage documentation connects Michele (Mike) and Ester as a couple and helps connect their individual histories to the Ronzitti family that later became part of The Panda story.

Why it matters: civil records can provide names, ages, residences, birthplaces and other details useful in tracing both families.

Naturalization

Michele (Mike) Ronzitti's naturalization documentation records his path to United States citizenship.

Why it matters: naturalization files can identify birth information, immigration details, residences, occupation and family relationships.

1950 Census

The 1950 United States Census records Michael and Ester Ronzitti with their daughters, Nicoletta Sema and Susan.

Why it matters: Ester's recorded age of approximately 34 supports a birth year around 1915–1916 and helps narrow the search for her birth documentation.

Sources & Verification

How this history is being reconstructed

Documented

Archival photographs, newspaper clippings, government records and surviving Panda ephemera are treated as documentary evidence.

Family History

Details including Michele (Mike), Andy and Gus acquiring The Panda together, Michele (Mike) later buying them out, and the Panda charms being handed out are presented as family-provided history unless independently documented.

Still Being Researched

Exact ownership dates, additional exterior photographs, interior photographs, a surviving menu and further business records remain active areas of research.

Storefront verification: the exterior photograph clearly shows the Panda name on the rounded canopy and “The Panda RESTAURANT” in the storefront window. The historical photograph is displayed without rewriting or reconstructing the signage.
Ongoing Research

Help us find more

We are continuing to search for photographs, menus, advertisements, matchbooks, postcards, newspaper stories, memorabilia and personal memories of The Panda Restaurant & Supper Club at 13 East 47th Street, particularly from approximately 1949–1951.

We are especially interested in interior photographs, additional exterior photographs, photographs of East 47th Street between Fifth and Madison Avenues, and an original Panda menu.

The archive will continue to change as additional material is located, evaluated and verified.

This site is a family historical research project. Family recollections are identified separately from documentary evidence whenever possible.
