A Family History

The story behind a small Midtown restaurant and supper club

In the years following World War II, The Panda operated at 13 East 47th Street, between Fifth and Madison Avenues in the heart of Midtown Manhattan.

Its history became intertwined with the life of Michele (Mike) Ronzitti, an Italian immigrant who built a life and family in New York City.

Family history records that Michele (Mike) Ronzitti entered the restaurant business with Andy and Gus. The three acquired an existing restaurant called The Panda and retained its name in hopes of keeping its established customers.

Michele (Mike) Ronzitti later bought out Andy and Gus and became sole proprietor.

Surviving restaurant material shows that The Panda presented itself as more than simply a place to eat. Its promotional material advertised cocktails, dinner, supper, music, entertainment and dancing — the language of a small Midtown restaurant and supper club.

One of the restaurant's specialties: surviving Panda matchbooks advertise “Shrimp à la Panda.” Locating a surviving menu remains one of this project's highest research priorities.

Restaurant Branding

A panda was part of the identity

Surviving matchbooks show that the restaurant did not rely on the name alone. A panda illustration formed part of its visual branding.

The matchbooks display the Panda name, the East 47th Street address, the ELdorado telephone number and the phrase “Shrimp à la Panda.”

Family Records

The people behind the restaurant

The story of The Panda is also the story of the family behind it. Photographs and surviving records help reconstruct the lives of Michele (Mike) and Ester Ronzitti and place the restaurant within their larger family history.

Family Photograph

Michele (Mike) and Ester Ronzitti

Michele (Mike) Ronzitti and his wife, Ester, whose lives eventually became intertwined with the story of The Panda at 13 East 47th Street.

Michele was born in Genoa, Italy, and Ester in Yekaterinburg, Russia. They built their life together in New York and raised two daughters, Nicoletta Sema and Susan.

For their family, The Panda was more than a Midtown restaurant and supper club. It became part of the Ronzitti family story.

Sources & Verification

How this history is being reconstructed

The history presented here draws from surviving family photographs, family testimony, census records, naturalization documents, period newspaper coverage, restaurant ephemera, matchbooks and archival photographs.

Research continues into The Panda's history, including the search for additional exterior and interior photographs, advertisements, entertainment coverage and a surviving restaurant menu.

Where information comes from family history rather than an independently verified documentary source, that distinction is preserved as part of the historical record.
