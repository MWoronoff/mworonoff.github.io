A New York Family Story

The Panda

A restaurant and supper club in Midtown Manhattan, the family behind it, and an ongoing effort to recover a nearly forgotten piece of New York history.

13 East 47th Street · New York City · Circa 1949–1951
The Panda at 13 East 47th Street. The rounded canopy bears “PANDA” and “PANDA RESTAURANT · 13,” while the storefront window carries a circular “The Panda RESTAURANT” sign. Photograph located in the Lloyd Acker collection, New York Public Library.
The Story

A supper club in postwar Manhattan

In the late 1940s and early 1950s, The Panda operated at 13 East 47th Street in Midtown Manhattan, on the north side of the block between Fifth and Madison Avenues.

Family history records that Michael “Mike” Ronzitti entered the restaurant business with Andy and Gus. The three acquired an existing restaurant called The Panda and retained its name in hopes of keeping its established customers.

Mike later bought out Andy and Gus and became sole proprietor. Surviving newspaper evidence confirms Mike's connection to the restaurant by 1951.

The Panda presented itself as more than a place to eat. Surviving promotional material advertised cocktails, dinner, supper, music, entertainment and dancing — the language of a small Midtown restaurant and supper club.

One of the restaurant's specialties: surviving Panda matchbooks advertise “Shrimp à la Panda.” Locating a surviving menu remains one of the project's highest research priorities.
Surviving Evidence

The Panda in its own words

One surviving promotional card provides one of the clearest contemporary records of the business.

It identifies The Panda Restaurant, Inc. at 13 East 47th Street, New York City and names Mike, Andy and Gus.

Cocktails · Dinner · Supper
Music · Entertainment · Dancing

The card also records the restaurant telephone number: ELdorado 5-7808.

Together with the surviving matchbooks, newspaper clippings and photographs, it establishes a recognizable identity for the restaurant during the period the Ronzitti family was involved.

Restaurant Branding

A panda was part of the identity

Surviving restaurant material shows that The Panda did not rely on its name alone. A panda illustration formed part of the restaurant's visual identity.

The matchbooks display the Panda name, East 47th Street address, ELdorado telephone number and the phrase “Shrimp à la Panda.”

That branding has become especially valuable in evaluating period photographs. A panda symbol, the rounded canopy or other distinctive storefront details may identify the restaurant even when lettering is difficult to read.

A Surviving Souvenir

The sterling Panda charm

Some guests left The Panda with more than a memory.

This small sterling silver panda survives as one of the most personal pieces of memorabilia connected with the supper club. According to family history, these panda charms were handed out at The Panda.

The surviving example remains attached to a backing card marked “Top Hat Charm” and “Sterling.” The number 1729 is visible at the bottom of the card.

Research is continuing into the manufacturer or distributor of the charm. Identifying the source may help date the object and reveal additional information about how it was used by the restaurant.

Family-provided provenance · manufacturer research ongoing
Family Records

The people behind the restaurant

The Panda story extends well beyond East 47th Street. Marriage, naturalization and census records help reconstruct Mike and Ester's lives and place the restaurant within the larger story of an immigrant family in New York.

Marriage record

The marriage documentation connects Mike and Ester as a couple and helps connect their individual histories to the Ronzitti family that would later become part of The Panda story.

Why it matters: Civil records can provide names, ages, residences, birthplaces and other details useful in tracing both families across immigration and census records.

Naturalization

Mike Ronzitti's naturalization documentation records his path to United States citizenship and provides an important link between Michele Ronzitti, the immigrant from Italy, and Michael “Mike” Ronzitti of New York.

Why it matters: Naturalization files may identify birth information, immigration details, residences, occupation and family relationships that help reconstruct Mike's life before The Panda.

1950 Census

The 1950 United States Census records Michael and Ester Ronzitti with their daughters, Nicoletta Sema and Susan. It gives us a snapshot of the family during The Panda years.

Why it matters: Ester's recorded age of approximately 34 supports a birth year around 1915–1916 and helps narrow the ongoing search for her birth documentation in Yekaterinburg.

Sources & Verification

How this history is being reconstructed

Not every detail survives in the same form. This project distinguishes between archival evidence, period documentation and family testimony — and avoids changing historical material simply to make it fit the story.

Documented

Archival photographs, newspaper clippings, government records and surviving Panda ephemera are treated as documentary evidence and identified as such wherever possible.

Family History

Details including Mike, Andy and Gus acquiring The Panda together, Mike later buying them out, and the sterling panda charms being handed out at the supper club are currently presented as family-provided history unless independently documented.

Still Being Researched

Exact ownership dates, additional exterior photographs, interior photographs, a surviving menu, business records and the manufacturer of the sterling panda charm remain active areas of research.

Storefront verification: the surviving exterior photograph shows “PANDA” on the rounded canopy, “PANDA RESTAURANT · 13” along its side and “The Panda RESTAURANT” in the circular storefront-window sign. The historical photograph is displayed without reconstructing or rewriting that signage.
Ongoing Research

Help us find more

We are continuing to search for photographs, menus, advertisements, matchbooks, postcards, newspaper stories, memorabilia and personal memories of The Panda Restaurant & Supper Club at 13 East 47th Street, particularly from approximately 1949–1951.

We are especially interested in interior photographs, additional exterior photographs, photographs of East 47th Street between Fifth and Madison Avenues, and an original Panda menu.

We are also searching for advertisements, employee photographs, entertainment listings, restaurant-supply records and memories from people who visited or worked at The Panda.

The archive will continue to change as additional material is located, evaluated and verified.

This site is a family historical research project. Family recollections are identified separately from documentary evidence whenever possible. Captions, dates and interpretations may be refined as additional primary-source evidence becomes available.
