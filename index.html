The Story

A supper club in postwar Manhattan

In the late 1940s and early 1950s, The Panda operated at 13 East 47th Street in Midtown Manhattan, on the north side of the block between Fifth and Madison Avenues.

Family history records that Michael “Mike” Ronzitti entered the restaurant business with Andy and Gus. The three acquired an existing restaurant called The Panda and retained its name in hopes of keeping its established customers. Mike later bought out Andy and Gus and became sole proprietor.

Surviving restaurant material shows that The Panda presented itself as more than a place to eat. Its own promotional card advertised cocktails, dinner, supper, music, entertainment and dancing — the language of a small Midtown supper club.

Surviving Evidence

The Panda in its own words

One surviving promotional card provides one of the clearest contemporary records of the business.

It identifies The Panda Restaurant, Inc. at 13 East 47th Street, New York City and names Mike, Andy and Gus.

Cocktails · Dinner · Supper
Music · Entertainment · Dancing

The card also records the restaurant telephone number: ELdorado 5-7808.

Restaurant Branding

A panda was part of the identity

The surviving matchbooks show that the restaurant did not rely on the name alone. A panda illustration formed part of its visual branding.

The matchbooks display the Panda name, the East 47th Street address, the ELdorado telephone number and the phrase “Shrimp à la Panda.”

This is especially useful when evaluating period photographs: a panda symbol may identify the restaurant even when its name cannot be read.

Timeline

What the evidence currently tells us

1941

The earlier Panda storefront

A New York City tax-era photograph shows the earlier establishment at 13 East 47th Street with its distinctive checkered awning. Family research identifies this as the Panda restaurant that Mike, Andy and Gus later acquired.

Late 1940s

Mike, Andy and Gus

A surviving Panda Restaurant card names Mike, Andy and Gus together at 13 East 47th Street and documents the restaurant's supper-club positioning.

Circa 1949–1951

Mike becomes sole proprietor

According to family testimony, Mike later bought out Andy and Gus. This ownership sequence remains part of the ongoing documentary research.

1951

Mike Ronzitti dies

Period newspaper evidence places Mike's death at The Panda in 1951.

Today

The reconstruction continues

Photographs, newspaper clippings, government records, restaurant ephemera and family testimony are being assembled into a single historical record.

Sources & Verification

How this history is being reconstructed

Not every detail survives in the same form. This project distinguishes between archival evidence, period documentation and family testimony.

Documented

Archival photographs, newspaper clippings, government records and surviving Panda ephemera are treated as documentary evidence and identified as such wherever possible.

Family History

Details such as Andy and Gus acquiring the restaurant with Mike, and Mike later buying them out, are currently presented as family-provided history unless independently documented.

Still Being Researched

Exact ownership dates, additional photographs, interior views, menus and other business records remain active areas of research.

Ongoing Research

Help us find more

We are continuing to search for photographs, menus, advertisements, matchbooks, postcards, newspaper stories and personal memories of The Panda Restaurant at 13 East 47th Street, particularly from approximately 1949–1951.

We are especially interested in exterior and interior photographs of The Panda, photographs of East 47th Street between Fifth and Madison Avenues, menus, advertisements, employee photographs and memories from people who visited or worked at the restaurant.

The archive will continue to change as additional material is located, evaluated and verified.

This site is a family historical research project. Captions and dates may be refined as additional primary-source evidence becomes available.
